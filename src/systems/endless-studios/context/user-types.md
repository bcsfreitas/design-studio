## About this document

In October 2026, ten people across Endless Access described eight user types in four fields: why they show up, needs, pain points, motivations (42 answers, two proposed missing types). This document merges those answers with the user interviews and transcripts, the team's own specifications and the Educator Research 2026 desk research, one section per type, in the same four fields. It then writes each type's jobs to be done, shows where the jobs overlap, and lists the shared jobs case by case.

### How to read the tags

Every point carries its origins, so a reader can tell a hypothesis from evidence without leaving the page.

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Team ×n` | n colleagues said it in the October 2026 intake. |
| `Voice` | A user said it: learner interviews and transcripts, educator usability sessions, the KIPP teacher interview. |
| `Docs` | A team document (onboarding specs, Educators Profiles, Partner Types, meeting notes, vault research syntheses), a partner report or measured program data. |
| `Desk` | Secondary research: Educator Research 2026, Findings by Educator Context. |

Team or Desk alone is a hypothesis. Docs makes it partial. Voice makes it evidence. The intake was written by colleagues in the user's voice, so on its own it is a team view, not user research; its value is where answers converge and where they agree with what users said and what the documents record.

### Confidence levels

- **Evidence-based**: interviews or usage data from Endless Access users.
- **Partial**: field observations, partner feedback or internal documents; no interviews with the role.
- **Hypothesized**: assumptions plus desk research. Treat as a research brief.

## Part 1 · The user types


### Young learner by choice

**Group:** Learner · **Intake:** 6 answers · **Confidence:** Partial

Through an after-school club, a summer camp, a youth centre or an open cohort. Brought by a partner, the account is institution-linked but works like a personal one; found alone, it is an Independent Learner account.

**Where the evidence comes from.** Team view agrees with field observations and the onboarding specs. No learner under 18 has been interviewed; the three student interview recordings from October are not transcribed yet.

#### Why they show up
- Likes games and wants to make one, not only play them. `Team ×5`
- Friends are going, and it does not feel like school: nobody grades, trying and failing is allowed. `Team ×3, Docs`
- An adult opened the door: a mentor, facilitator, parent or teacher suggested it, "but maybe I still took the choice". `Team ×2, Docs`
- A line for a college application, a certificate, or just something to do. `Team ×1`

#### Needs
- Finish a game that is theirs, with their own characters and story, and show it to friends and family at a closing event. `Team ×4, Docs`
- A visible result fast. The onboarding spec makes the first win the last step of onboarding, seen and liked by cohort peers. `Team ×2, Docs`
- Proof: a certificate, the ASU microcredential, a letter of recommendation. `Team ×2, Docs`
- Art, music and self-expression, not only code. `Team ×2, Docs`
- Identity as a reward: a custom username and avatar unlock only after parental consent; before that, an assigned name and a preset avatar. `Docs`
- Learn to use AI to help build the game. `Team ×1`

#### Pain points
- Real code and the tools are hard at the start; game problems have many steps; it is a lot at once. `Team ×3, Docs`
- Devices and access: a slow laptop, a phone at home when the game needs a keyboard, data that runs out, a PC that cannot run Endstar. Work happens only at the centre. `Team ×4, Docs`
- The first 10 to 15 minutes go to installing Godot or hunting an access code read aloud instead of shown on screen. `Docs`
- Miss a session and lose the thread. The home checklist in the onboarding spec exists so a returning learner has not "lost the thread". `Team ×2, Docs`
- Fear of mistakes, low frustration tolerance, low confidence. `Team ×2, Docs`
- Consent before participation: a minor who joins an Endless-run open cohort or workshop alone meets the parental-consent gate at join, not only at publish. `Team ×1, Docs`
- Transport to the venue; limited scheduling. `Team ×2`
- The AI helper does not understand what they are trying to do. `Team ×1`

#### Motivations
- The moment the game works, and watching other people play it. `Team ×4, Docs`
- A mentor who helps fast, checks in and remembers what they are building. `Team ×4, Docs`
- Friends, peers and their validation. Food at the event. `Team ×3`
- Small wins and scaffolding that guides the journey. `Team ×1, Docs`
- A certificate to show at home or use for college. `Team ×2, Docs`

#### Jobs to be done

Format: When [situation/context], I want to [motivation/action], so I can [expected progress or outcome].

- **Functional.** When I come to the club after school, I want to make a game that is mine and finish it before the program ends, so I can show it at the closing event. `Team ×4, Docs`
- **Functional.** When I miss a week, I want to pick up where my project was, so I do not fall behind and forget what I was doing. `Team ×2, Docs`
- **Functional.** When I only have a phone at home, I want my work to be safe at the centre and ready next time, so the session is not spent recovering it. `Team ×4, Docs`
- **Emotional.** When I try something and it breaks, I want to fix it without being graded or judged, so I keep trying. `Team ×3, Docs`
- **Emotional.** When I am stuck, I want help fast from someone who remembers my project, so I do not lose the session. `Team ×4, Docs`
- **Social.** When the game works, I want friends and family to play it and react, so I count as someone who makes games. `Team ×4, Docs`
- **Social.** When the program ends, I want a certificate I can show at home or put on a college application, so the time counts. `Team ×2, Docs`

### Young learner at school, as part of the school day

**Group:** Learner · **Intake:** 7 answers (one with two fields only) · **Confidence:** Partial

Because the teacher or school runs the program. Usually an NDPA-restricted account the institution owns: no self-registration, no platform terms, no age check; today it can enrol only in Explore: Threadbare.

**Where the evidence comes from.** Team view agrees with partner-site observations and the onboarding specs. No learner under 18 has been interviewed.

#### Why they show up
- They have to. It is on the timetable, often graded; they did not choose it. The institution provisions the account. `Team ×6, Docs`
- Still, it is the hands-on lesson: building something of their own, "more fun than other topics". `Team ×4`
- Sometimes framed by the teacher as a way to express or solve a real-world issue. `Team ×1`

#### Needs
- Finish what the teacher asks, pass the skills tasks and the capstone, get the grade. `Team ×4`
- If there is time, add their own ideas, make the game better than the example, say something with the story. `Team ×3`
- Learn something new; go deeper on the parts they find interesting, like sound. `Team ×2`
- Understand how the mark is worked out. `Team ×1`
- Find the class without the teacher dictating URLs, submit without leaving the classroom flow, "come back tomorrow and not feel lost", and not be shown things they cannot access. `Docs`
- Introduce themselves safely: a structured, skippable prompt seen only in the cohort; no open bio for NDPA minors. `Docs`
- Learn how AI works by using it to make things. `Team ×1`

#### Pain points
- School devices and networks: slow shared Chromebooks, blocked sites, software that cannot be installed, IT that cannot troubleshoot, no computer or internet at home. `Team ×5, Docs`
- Logins eat the lesson; some schools do not let students use email on unknown sites. `Team ×2, Docs`
- Class ends when they get into it; work does not save or cannot be found next time; levels cannot be copied or reused. `Team ×2, Docs`
- School, work and family compete with session time: the most cited reason for missing sessions across partner cohorts. `Docs`
- One teacher for 25 to 30 students; a loud room; help is scarce. `Team ×2`
- Not enough class time to learn the creator tools. `Team ×2, Docs`
- Endstar path: too much content on the platform, the download is hard to find, Endless and Endstar names are mixed, the launcher is an extra step, the wiki lives elsewhere, error messages are cryptic. `Team ×1`
- Feedback on their work goes unseen because notifications are off or easy to miss. A partner warned that teenagers treat platforms like this as homework. `Docs`

#### Motivations
- Making something that is theirs; a feeling of progression. `Team ×4, Docs`
- Playtesting and building with friends, in teams. `Team ×3, Docs`
- Presenting: a pitch, a class showcase, a competition judged by real people. `Team ×2`
- An encouraging teacher, feedback, and help when stuck. `Team ×2, Docs`
- Seeing progress; a certificate or microcredential. `Team ×2, Docs`
- Being good at something that does not feel like regular school. `Team ×1`

#### Jobs to be done

Format: When [situation/context], I want to [motivation/action], so I can [expected progress or outcome].

- **Functional.** When the lesson starts, I want to be in my work within minutes without logins and installs eating the period, so I use the class time I have. `Team ×2, Docs`
- **Functional.** When the teacher sets the task, I want to finish it and know how my mark is worked out, so I pass. `Team ×4`
- **Functional.** When class ends mid-task, I want to come back next lesson and find my work, so I do not start over. `Team ×2, Docs`
- **Emotional.** When I have to be here, I want it to feel like building something real, not homework, so I care about it. `Team ×4, Docs`
- **Emotional.** When the one teacher is busy with 25 others, I want a way to get unstuck on my own, so I do not sit idle. `Team ×2`
- **Social.** When I share unfinished work, I want only my class and teacher to see it, so I can try things in front of people I know. `Docs`
- **Social.** When we present, I want my game played and judged by real people, so I am good at something that is not regular school. `Team ×2`

### Adult learner

**Group:** Learner · **Intake:** 7 answers (one describes a college club member, see Across types) · **Confidence:** Evidence-based

Joining alongside studies or a job, by choice or for course credit. Many are not computing students: business, marketing, industrial and biomedical engineering.

**Where the evidence comes from.** Fourteen or more UTP learner interviews (GameLab 4.0 and 5), thirteen GameLab Peru alumni and spotlight transcripts and one Lore Jam graduate. Team view matches them closely. One institution, mostly engineering students.

#### Why they show up
- Try the field and find out if a career in games or tech is for them; widen professional options. `Team ×6, Voice`
- Course credit, a certificate or a microcredential. `Team ×4, Voice`
- A university email, often ignored several times before it lands; a classmate; an end-of-year showcase. `Team ×2, Voice`
- A hobby outside a structured day job: a graduate with an office job did the Lore Jam in his own time, "nos dimos ese tiempo para hacerlo". `Team ×1, Voice`
- A game course cut from their study plan; GameLab filled the gap. `Voice`
- Paid bounties, via a college club. A different motive; see Across types. `Team ×1`

#### Needs
- Learn tools the industry uses, like Godot, and get a real introduction to game making. `Team ×4, Voice`
- A portfolio piece and a credential to show an employer or put on LinkedIn. `Team ×4, Voice`
- See the path: what comes next, which career fits. Alumni ask for a second course or a way to finish the game; self-guided programs are still "coming soon". `Team ×2, Voice, Docs`
- Know Threadbare's limits before designing: no custom menus, family-friendly content rules, free-licence assets only. Teams were recalled after delivery to swap assets. `Voice`
- A playable prototype early; mentors ask for one before approving a new mechanic. `Voice`
- Finish together with a team. `Voice`
- Quick sign-up and a clear view of what is on offer before committing. `Team ×1`

#### Pain points
- Time. Work, studies, caretaking. If stuck, this drops down the priority list. `Team ×6, Voice`
- Creator tools are hard; Git is the top breaking point in interviews, and it breaks the project, not only the person: "rompí todos los códigos". `Team ×2, Voice`
- Language: materials, Git guides and instructions in English only; learners translate as they go. `Team ×1, Voice`
- No computer at home; joining from a phone means missing activities. `Team ×2, Voice`
- Not sure the credential is recognised outside the program. In the transcripts no learner mentions the certificate; recognition came through a job interview, a paid internship and the credits. `Team ×1, Voice`
- Little visible progress for the little time invested; "I didn't know what I didn't know". `Team ×1, Voice`
- The team goes quiet around week two; five become two; teams move from Discord to WhatsApp to talk at all. A late joiner from a broken link feared not knowing where the group was. `Voice`
- Slow registration, forms, contracts, terms of service; no approachable bounties. `Team ×1`

#### Motivations
- Mentors and the team: the top motivator in interviews (92.9%). `Team ×3, Voice`
- Certificates and credit for the portfolio, LinkedIn or academic credit; sharing success on social media. `Team ×4, Voice`
- Shipping something real and seeing it played: at the closing event, and by children in New York playing a quest in Spanish. `Team ×2, Voice`
- Their name in the credits of a real game. `Voice`
- A clear line from learning to a job: a paid internship nobody expected, a first tech job, alumni returning as mentors. `Team ×2, Voice`
- Seeing their skills progress; small meaningful tasks finished; swapping roles with teammates. `Team ×2, Voice`

#### Jobs to be done

Format: When [situation/context], I want to [motivation/action], so I can [expected progress or outcome].

- **Functional.** When I invest my scarce time, I want to learn tools the industry uses and ship something real, so it counts for a portfolio and a job. `Team ×4, Voice`
- **Functional.** When Git breaks my project, I want to recover without starting over, so a week of work is not lost. `Team ×2, Voice`
- **Functional.** When the program ends, I want to know the next step, a second course, finishing the game or a credential employers recognise, so the momentum is not wasted. `Team ×2, Voice, Docs`
- **Emotional.** When my team goes quiet, I want to still have something to contribute and someone to talk to, so I do not quit silently. `Voice`
- **Emotional.** When the materials are in English, I want to follow in my own language, so I do not fall behind translating. `Team ×1, Voice`
- **Social.** When the game ships, I want my name in the credits and strangers playing it, so I am a game developer, not a student. `Team ×2, Voice`
- **Social.** When I finish, I want a credential for LinkedIn and contact with people in the industry, so the course opens a door. `Team ×4, Voice`

| Show the work to people who matter | Core | the game ships | my name in the credits and strangers playing it, in my language | be a game developer, not a student |

| Fit it into the time I have | Core | work, studies and family compete | to progress in the slots I have | keep this on my priority list |

### Autonomous classroom educator

**Group:** Teacher or facilitator · **Intake:** 6 answers (one with one field; one describes a college club leader) · **Confidence:** Evidence-based

Choosing to bring Endless into a class, elective or club, through a partner network or after finding it online. In the US and Canada, usually an experienced teacher new to computer science.

**Where the evidence comes from.** Thin but firsthand: five website usability sessions with educators (Jan–Feb 2026, not yet users) and one interview with a KIPP teacher who runs Endless programs.

#### Why they show up
- Game making engages learners better than most material; it is multidisciplinary and gives students a new way to express what they know, even in Literature. `Team ×4, Voice, Docs`
- It widens who takes part: girls, and students who struggle in maths. Electives compete for student time, so the course has to be "worth their while". `Voice`
- A dead calendar window to fill: "Eighteen sessions is basically what I've got after the AP exam." `Voice`
- Git itself is a hook: "Nobody teaches Git in high school." `Voice`
- Often the only CS teacher in the building, searching for material alone. Half of US CS teachers have no CS colleague; 81% came to CS without a technical background. `Team ×1, Desk`
- A free tool that non-technical students can use; a platform that shows progress and may automate assessment. `Team ×1`

#### Needs
- Ready-to-teach resources to preview, adapt and get approved: lesson plans, pacing, a way to show learning. `Team ×2, Voice, Docs`
- See one lesson before creating an account. All five usability participants stopped at sign-up: "Let me see one lesson before I sign up." `Voice`
- Plain device and tool facts on the page: "runs on Chromebooks, yes or no", which Godot version, whether Discord can be skipped, whether Threadbare is finished or pre-alpha. `Voice`
- Units that fit a period or two; cross-curricular hooks (writing students do dialogue, design students do assets); a "Not a CS teacher? Start here" entry. `Voice, Desk`
- Predictable pull-request review turnaround inside a 16-week semester. `Voice`
- Help teaching AI: 81% of US CS teachers think AI belongs in the course, 42% feel equipped. `Desk`
- Give students more opportunities: a new field, technical skills, programming taught in an engaging way; a real audience beyond the classroom. `Team ×3`

#### Pain points
- Approval: district privacy reviews, DPAs, software whitelisting, blocked tools, Chromebooks that cannot run everything. "The district privacy person was in my inbox three days later." `Team ×3, Voice, Desk`
- Own lack of game-design confidence; needs extra help. "Me not being a coder." `Team ×2, Voice, Desk`
- Age floors exclude middle school: Explore 13+, Core 15+. "It's the one I want most and it's the one I can't use." `Voice`
- Credential rules differ by page: "There's like three versions?" Skills and achievements are "coming soon" on the platform. `Voice`
- The platform speaks to a learner, not a teacher: no educator role at sign-up, "My Programs", an access code lost if they leave the flow, an educator space showing "0 Facilitators". Educators cannot create cohorts. `Docs`
- Endstar: technical issues, few guides, a separate old-looking wiki, school-managed PCs that do not update, no in-app release warning. `Team ×1`
- Younger learners: under-13 submissions have to be worked around; offensive user-made content; NPCs attack by default. `Team ×2`
- Godot in a classroom: dark editor on projectors, browser projects lost unless downloaded, no curriculum or classroom tools, and the Godot project cannot sign a DPA. `Voice, Desk`

#### Motivations
- Students who switch on, stay after class, and leave the room with exciting questions; a shy student who became club president and showcase MC. `Team ×4, Voice`
- The ASU credential as a pitch to students: "they'd go nuts for that. College apps." `Voice`
- Contributing to a real game: "you're gonna add a level to this. That's the pitch"; open-source work as "a job skill I can't fake". `Voice`
- Other teachers to swap ideas and fixes with; coaching after onboarding. 84% of US CS teachers belong to CSTA and look outward for peers. `Team ×1, Desk`
- A responsive Endstar team; new releases arriving. `Team ×1`

#### Jobs to be done

Format: When [situation/context], I want to [motivation/action], so I can [expected progress or outcome].

- **Functional.** When I evaluate a program, I want to see one lesson and the facts on devices, age and credentials before creating an account, so I can decide in minutes. `Voice`
- **Functional.** When I plan the semester, I want ready-to-teach units that fit a period and a pacing guide, so I am not building from scratch. `Team ×2, Voice, Desk`
- **Functional.** When the district asks, I want privacy paperwork someone can sign and a setup that runs on Chromebooks, so approval does not take months. `Team ×3, Voice, Desk`
- **Emotional.** When I am not a coder, I want to run a good session anyway, so I never improvise in front of 30 teenagers. `Team ×2, Voice, Desk`
- **Emotional.** When I am the only CS teacher in the building, I want peers who have taught this, so I am not alone with it. `Team ×1, Voice, Desk`
- **Social.** When students ship, I want a credential they can put on college applications and work I can show the school, so the elective earns its place. `Team ×1, Voice`

#### Shared jobs this type has (11 of 13)

| Shared job | Weight | When | I want | So I can |

### Mandate-driven classroom educator

**Group:** Teacher or facilitator · **Intake:** 5 answers (one repeats the previous type) · **Confidence:** Partial

Teaching Endless within the timetable because a ministry, district or school decided the curriculum. UAE, Saudi Arabia, Jordan and Mexico are the documented cases.

**Where the evidence comes from.** Team view plus the Jordan partner report and desk research. No teacher in this situation has been interviewed; the desk research found no Saudi teacher voices either.

#### Why they show up
- Assigned. The school, district or ministry committed to the program, or the national curriculum now asks for digital skills; leadership expects results. `Team ×5, Docs, Desk`
- AI is now compulsory: the UAE from 2025–26, taught within existing hours by designated teachers; Saudi Arabia from 2025–26; Jordan's digital-skills curriculum for grades 1 to 12. `Desk`
- Mexico's Nueva Escuela Mexicana mandates project-based learning, the closest fit to game making. `Desk`
- Wants to do it well even without having chosen it. `Team ×2`

#### Needs
- Deliver every lesson on time and as designed; keep the whole class engaged. `Team ×4`
- Extra help understanding game design and the tools; they are not game developers. `Team ×3, Docs`
- Assess games and pitches fairly and enter marks that line up with the authority's framework. `Team ×1`
- Materials in their own language. Arabic is under 10% of online content; educator-facing Spanish Godot material is almost absent. `Team ×1, Desk`
- Training that reaches them through the system: supervisors first, then teachers. Jordan trained 136 supervisors and about 6,500 teachers that way. `Desk`
- Show the supervisor or director that it is working. `Team ×1, Docs`

#### Pain points
- Not a CS teacher, not a game developer; one step ahead of the class; no time to practise before teaching. In Mexico only 20% felt prepared for the new curriculum. `Team ×4, Docs, Desk`
- Devices, logins and weak connectivity eat lesson time. 42% of Mexican teachers could not use their own ministry's digital resources. `Team ×2, Docs, Desk`
- Grading games by hand takes hours; reports do not use the authority's vocabulary. `Team ×1, Docs`
- Training is one workshop with no follow-up; Jordanian teachers "asked repeatedly for follow-up" and objected to after-hours training and travel. `Team ×1, Desk`
- No freedom to change the curriculum; long approval chains; stricter data and whitelisting rules. Adoption is decided by the system and its implementing partners. `Team ×2, Desk`

### What the overlap says

- **Three jobs run through every type.** Showing the work, getting help without being alone, and proof that counts outside appear in all eight, learners and adults alike. Whatever serves these serves everyone; they are the cheapest places to invest.
- **Learner and educator jobs are the same data seen from two sides.** "Keep the thread between sessions" for the learner is "see who is stuck" for the educator and "attainment by site" for the partner. One progress record, three views. The same holds for showing the work: the learner's showcase is the partner's story, and the release form sits between them.
- **Adults share a job learners never have: run it without me.** Teachers new to CS, facilitators with no prep time, parents who cannot code and partners who want programs to run without Endless staff all ask for the same thing in different words: materials and training that make the person in the room optional. Self-serve facilitator training and turnkey sessions serve four types at once.
- **Some jobs belong to one type only.** Preview before sign-up (autonomous educator), stand-alone sessions for drop-ins (facilitator), a transcript line from logged hours (independent educator), the authority's vocabulary in reports (mandate-driven educator and partner), name in the credits (adult learner). These are where the types differ and where one-size material fails.
- **Three jobs pull against each other.** The young learner by choice wants strangers to play the game; the school learner wants only the class to see it; the partner wants nothing to leave the cohort without consent. The educator wants to see a lesson before an account exists; the team wants the account to measure impact. The adult wants industry tools; the mandate teacher wants nothing new. Each is a product decision, not a copy problem.

## Part 4 · Shared jobs, case by case

The thirteen shared jobs, each written per user type as: When [situation/context], I want to [motivation/action], So I can [expected progress or outcome]. ● core for the type, ○ present. The same statements appear inside each type in Part 1; this view groups them by job.


### Show the work to people who matter

Closing events, pitches, playtests, credits; for adults, showcases, stories and footage.

- ● **Young learner by choice** — When the closing event comes, I want friends and family to play the game I made, so I can count as someone who makes games.
- ● **Young learner at school** — When we present to the class or enter a competition, I want my game played and judged by real people, so I can be good at something that is not regular school.
- ● **Adult learner** — When the game ships, I want my name in the credits and strangers playing it, in my language, so I can be a game developer, not a student.
- ● **Autonomous educator** — When the elective ends, I want student work I can show the school and students can put on college applications, so I can make the course earn its place next year.
- ● **Mandate-driven educator** — When the term ends, I want a pitch or showcase my supervisor and ministry can see, so I can have my delivery recognised.
- ● **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When the cohort closes, I want a showcase where families and the community see what learners built, so I can give my organization and its funders a result they can see.
- ○ **Independent non-formal educator** — When the kids finish something, I want them to show it to someone beyond me, so I can keep them asking for more.
- ● **Reporting partner or buyer** — When we report outward, I want stories, footage and student work with our name on them, so I can show our board, donors and public the impact.

### Get help when stuck, and not be alone with it

A mentor who remembers the project; peers who have taught it; someone to call; a parent community.

- ● **Young learner by choice** — When I am stuck mid-session, I want fast help from a mentor who remembers my project, so I can not lose the session.
- ● **Young learner at school** — When the only teacher is busy with 25 others, I want a way to get unstuck on my own, so I can not sit idle until the bell.
- ● **Adult learner** — When my team goes quiet or Git breaks the project, I want a mentor or teammate to turn to the same day, so I can not quit silently.
- ● **Autonomous educator** — When I am the only CS teacher in the building, I want peers who have taught this and coaching after onboarding, so I can not be alone with it.
- ● **Mandate-driven educator** — When I am one step ahead of the class, I want training that comes back after the first workshop and colleagues who share resources, so I can not be left to improvise.
- ● **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When something breaks mid-session, I want someone to call, a trainer or a WhatsApp group, so I can not lose the room.
- ● **Independent non-formal educator** — When I do not know how to code, I want someone to ask and other parents and mentors to compare notes with, so I can not give up.
- · Not a job for: Reporting partner or buyer.

### Proof that counts outside

Certificate or microcredential for learners; the teacher's own credential; a transcript line; attainment for the buyer.

- ○ **Young learner by choice** — When the program ends, I want a certificate, so I can show it at home or on a college application.
- ○ **Young learner at school** — When the course is graded, I want a mark I understand and a microcredential, so I can have it count for school.
- ● **Adult learner** — When I finish, I want a credential employers recognise and a portfolio piece, so I can open a door to a job.
- ● **Autonomous educator** — When students ship, I want a credential they can put on college applications, so I can make the elective worth their while.
- ● **Mandate-driven educator** — When I deliver the program, I want a microcredential for my own delivery and recognition from the ministry, so I can have it count for me too.
- ○ **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When learners finish, I want proof of participation we can count, so I can let the organization report it.
- ● **Independent non-formal educator** — When my teen finishes, I want logged hours and a course description for a transcript, so I can have the work count as credit.
- ● **Reporting partner or buyer** — When the contract closes, I want attainment in our framework and our bands, so I can prove value for money to my leadership.

### Make something real and finish it

The learner's core job; the adults' reason to run it.

- ● **Young learner by choice** — When I come to the club, I want to make a game that is mine and finish it before the program ends, so I can have something to show.
- ● **Young learner at school** — When the teacher sets the task, I want to finish it and, if there is time, add my own ideas, so I can pass, and still have it be mine.
- ● **Adult learner** — When I invest my scarce time, I want to ship something real with tools the industry uses, so I can have it count for a portfolio.
- ○ **Autonomous educator** — When I run the unit, I want every student to leave with a working game, so I can let the engagement show in the result.
- ○ **Mandate-driven educator** — When the term ends, I want every learner, including those who never built anything, to have made a game, so I can meet the mandate visibly.
- ○ **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When the cohort ends, I want learners to leave with something concrete they are proud of, so I can have them come back and have something to count.
- ○ **Independent non-formal educator** — When we work week to week, I want my kids to finish something playable, so I can let them see the point.
- · Not a job for: Reporting partner or buyer.

### Start within minutes, not after logins and installs

The first session, the first lesson, the first day of a rollout.

- ● **Young learner by choice** — When the session starts, I want to be building, not installing, so I can make the two hours count.
- ● **Young learner at school** — When the lesson starts, I want to be in my work within minutes, without logins and installs, so I can use the class time I have.
- ○ **Adult learner** — When I join late or from a phone, I want to find where the group is, so I can not be lost from day one.
- ● **Autonomous educator** — When I evaluate a program, I want to see one lesson and the device, age and credential facts before creating an account, so I can decide in minutes.
- ● **Mandate-driven educator** — When devices, logins and connectivity fail, I want a lesson that still starts, so I can not lose the period.
- ● **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When I walk in with little prep, I want a turnkey session, so I can run it alone from the first minute.
- ● **Reporting partner or buyer** — When the first day approaches, I want every student in the right class with nothing to configure, so I can make the first session about learning, not access.
- · Not a job for: Independent non-formal educator.

### See where things stand

The learner's own progress; who is stuck in the class; attainment by site.

- ○ **Young learner by choice** — When I come back, I want to see my progress, so I can know I am getting somewhere.
- ○ **Young learner at school** — When I am being graded, I want to see how I am doing against the tasks, so I can not be surprised by the mark.
- ○ **Adult learner** — When I can only put in a little time, I want visible progress, so I can not lose heart.
- ● **Autonomous educator** — When students are working, I want to see who is stuck without asking one by one, so I can help before they give up.
- ● **Mandate-driven educator** — When I grade, I want progress and marks in the authority's vocabulary, without hours by hand, so I can defend the marks.
- ● **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When the cohort runs, I want who is progressing and who is stuck, on a roster I manage, so I can not depend on Endless for every change.
- ● **Reporting partner or buyer** — When programs run across sites, I want reach, completion and drop-off by site, so I can see results before the end.
- · Not a job for: Independent non-formal educator.

### Fit it into the time I have

Short sessions, class ending mid-task, adults' obligations, teachers' prep time.

- ○ **Young learner by choice** — When sessions are short and getting there is hard, I want each session to end with something done, so I can not have one missed week undo the rest.
- ● **Young learner at school** — When class ends mid-task, I want to come back next lesson and find my work, so I can not start over.
- ● **Adult learner** — When work, studies and family compete, I want to progress in the slots I have, so I can keep this on my priority list.
- ● **Autonomous educator** — When I have eighteen sessions after the AP exam, I want units that fit a period or two, so I can use the window I have.
- ● **Mandate-driven educator** — When I have no time to practise, I want lessons I can deliver as designed, so I can be prepared without extra nights.
- ● **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When I have little prep and kids come and go, I want sessions that stand alone, so I can not lose drop-ins by week three.
- · Not a job for: Independent non-formal educator, Reporting partner or buyer.

### Run it without being the expert, or without extra hands

Teachers new to CS, facilitators with no prep, parents who cannot code, partners without Endless staff.

- ● **Autonomous educator** — When I am not a coder, I want to run a good session anyway, so I can never improvise in front of 30 teenagers.
- ● **Mandate-driven educator** — When I am not a CS teacher, I want materials that carry the lesson, so I can deliver it as designed.
- ● **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When I am alone in the room, I want a session that handles group dynamics for me, so I can make it work without Endless staff.
- ● **Independent non-formal educator** — When I cannot code, I want to stay a few weeks ahead of my learner, so I can keep guiding.
- ● **Reporting partner or buyer** — When a program runs, I want it to run without Endless staff and replicate elsewhere, so I can scale it at a lower cost per learner.
- · Not a job for: Young learner by choice, Young learner at school, Adult learner.

### Get it approved and keep kids safe

District paperwork, whitelisting, consent, who can contact learners, data residency.

- ○ **Young learner by choice** — When I join alone as a minor, I want the parent consent handled without making it weird, so I can take part.
- ○ **Young learner at school** — When I share unfinished work, I want only my class and my teacher to see it, so I can try things in front of people I know.
- ● **Autonomous educator** — When the district asks, I want privacy paperwork someone can sign and clear answers on who can contact students, so I can get approval in weeks, not months.
- ● **Mandate-driven educator** — When the system decides, I want the software whitelisted and the data rules met, so I can be allowed to use it.
- ● **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When a parent asks who their kid talks to, I want a real answer and clear rules for under-13s, so I can keep their trust.
- ● **Independent non-formal educator** — When I pick a platform for kids, I want to know who can contact them and what they can reach, so I can trust it.
- ● **Reporting partner or buyer** — When a minor's data is involved, I want residency, ownership and consent answered, so I can have nothing come back as an incident.
- · Not a job for: Adult learner.

### Work on the devices and in the language we have

Phones, Chromebooks, blocked networks; Spanish, Arabic, local examples.

- ● **Young learner by choice** — When I only have a phone at home, I want my work safe at the centre, so I can not lose progress.
- ● **Young learner at school** — When Chromebooks are shared and sites are blocked, I want the tools to run anyway, so I can have the lesson happen.
- ● **Adult learner** — When materials are in English, I want to follow in my own language, so I can not fall behind translating.
- ○ **Autonomous educator** — When the lab is Chromebooks, I want a setup that runs on them, so I can be able to adopt it at all.
- ● **Mandate-driven educator** — When connectivity is weak and materials are in English, I want local-language lessons that work offline, so I can have my learners follow.
- ● **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When learners are on phones with intermittent data, I want materials that do not assume a laptop per learner, so I can make the session work.
- ○ **Independent non-formal educator** — When we have five computers for thirteen kids, I want activities that share machines, so I can have everyone take part.
- · Not a job for: Reporting partner or buyer.

### Keep the thread between sessions

Come back and find the work; drop-in groups; late joiners.

- ● **Young learner by choice** — When I miss a week, I want to pick up where my project was, so I can not fall behind.
- ● **Young learner at school** — When class ends mid-task, I want to find my work next lesson, so I can not start over.
- ○ **Adult learner** — When I join late, I want to know where the group is, so I can not be afraid of being lost.
- ● **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When kids drop in and out, I want each session to stand alone and each learner to find their place, so I can have nobody lost by week three.
- · Not a job for: Autonomous educator, Mandate-driven educator, Independent non-formal educator, Reporting partner or buyer.

### Know what comes next

After the program, after the first course, after this term.

- ○ **Young learner by choice** — When the program ends, I want to know how to keep making games, so I can not stop at the certificate.
- ○ **Young learner at school** — When the unit ends, I want a way to keep going outside class, so I can not have it be homework that ended.
- ● **Adult learner** — When the program ends, I want a second course, a way to finish the game or a path to a job, so I can not waste the momentum.
- ○ **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When the cohort ends, I want to run the next one myself, so I can not wait on Endless next year.
- ● **Independent non-formal educator** — When the first course ends, I want a path to continue, so I can not stall.
- ○ **Reporting partner or buyer** — When a pilot works, I want a clear path to scale, so I can make renewal an easy decision.
- · Not a job for: Autonomous educator, Mandate-driven educator.

### Belong to a group that keeps going

Friends and teams for learners; peers, trainers and WhatsApp groups for adults.

- ● **Young learner by choice** — When my friends are going, I want to build alongside them, so I can have it feel like us, not school.
- ○ **Young learner at school** — When we work in teams, I want to build and playtest with friends, so I can have class feel like making, not homework.
- ● **Adult learner** — When the team is active, I want us to finish together, so I can be proud of something we made.
- ○ **Autonomous educator** — When I am the only CS teacher, I want a community of teachers who have taught this, so I can not be alone.
- ● **Mandate-driven educator** — When everyone is rushed and overwhelmed, I want colleagues who share resources, so I can get through it together.
- ○ **Partner facilitator or program leader** — When I run sessions alone, I want a trainer or a WhatsApp group to lean on, so I can not be isolated.
- ○ **Independent non-formal educator** — When I guide without a school, I want other parents and mentors, so I can compare notes.
- · Not a job for: Reporting partner or buyer.
